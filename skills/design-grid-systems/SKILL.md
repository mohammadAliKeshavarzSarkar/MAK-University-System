# Learn — Grid Systems and Composition

**Status:** Learning skill; grids are design tools, not mandatory templates.  
**Study references:** [Poster House — The Swiss Grid](https://swissgrid.posterhouse.org/) and [Niggli — Grid Systems in Graphic Design](https://niggli.ch/en/products/rastersysteme-fur-die-visuelle-gestaltung). Consult [source limits](../design-learning/SOURCES.md).

## Trigger and goal

Use when studying layout, designing columns, arranging text with diagrams, reviewing balance/alignment, or comparing grid alternatives. Teach a grid as an intentional relationship among margins, columns, gutters and modular fields, not as visible boxes to fill.

## Source understanding

- Poster House surveys the history and impact of the Swiss Grid and presents alternative or altered-grid examples. It is historical/contextual, not a universal grid prescription.
- Niggli describes Müller-Brockmann's 1981 handbook and its 8–32-field applications to graphic and exhibition design. The accessible catalog **does not give us the complete book's specific chapter instructions**.
- The following 16:9 experiments are FRAME interpretations, **not original book measurements**.

## Exercise method

1. State the communicative task: define terms, compare items, show an algorithm, show data, or invite reflection.
2. Inventory the required items: headline, content, diagram, labels, equations, source credit and speaker notes.
3. Sketch at least two realistic arrangements (for example 2 columns vs 4 underlying columns), checking margin and gutter consistency.
4. Let a diagram span more fields if it carries the central concept. Do not force every item into a single module.
5. In Persian slides, test the **physical right-to-left order** of sequence nodes; object order and paragraph alignment are separate.
6. Render both at presentation size and compare reading path, clear semantic grouping, diagram legibility, whitespace and unused space.
7. Identify one reason to keep the grid and one reason to break it deliberately.

## Controlled mini-experiment

Reuse the same correct Q/K/V explanation in **three** layouts: text-led, balanced, and diagram-led. Hold title, terminology, brand palette, fonts, reference credit and factual content constant. Vary only spatial allocation. Evaluate which layout matches the learning objective; do not announce one as globally superior.

## Quality gate

- Can a viewer find the main claim quickly?
- Are aligned objects genuinely related?
- Are formulas, connectors and annotations readable at full-slide scale?
- Is a red/high-contrast item highlighted for a valid semantic reason?
- Does the reading sequence make sense in Persian as well as English?
- Are source claims separated from our PowerPoint-specific adaptations?

## Output and next step

Provide a short explanation, one or more comparison sketches (or editable slide if requested), a rationale for the chosen grid, and unresolved questions. If the user's task is building a deck, hand off to [FRAME production](../frame-presentations/SKILL.md); do not add mandatory column counts to the guidebook from a single sample.
