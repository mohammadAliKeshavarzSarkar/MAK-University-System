# Learn — Visual Hierarchy within a Grid

**Status:** Learning and comparison skill; no mandatory layout imposed.

## Reference boundary

Study examples and flexible grid thinking at [Poster House — The Swiss Grid](https://swissgrid.posterhouse.org/) and the [Niggli Müller-Brockmann handbook description](https://niggli.ch/en/products/rastersysteme-fur-die-visuelle-gestaltung). Carbon's [typography overview](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/typography/overview) explains how type styles and weights distinguish hierarchy in its UI system. The five principles and QA below are FRAME applications, not verbatim claims from a book.

## What to teach

- **Scale:** Make the primary learning question and central mechanism clearly dominant.
- **Contrast:** Separate key ideas from support without implying false scientific importance.
- **Alignment:** Align things that belong together and distinguish things that do not.
- **Whitespace:** Use spacing for grouping and focus rather than decoration.
- **Semantic truth:** The highest-contrast feature must have a legitimate communicative purpose.

## Controlled before/after practice

Take one existing slide and fix the content, formula, terminology, diagram topology, MAK palette and fonts. First describe the current eye path; then redesign only hierarchy and spacing. In the previous Q/K/V exercise, highlighting Query in Signal red wrongly implied stronger importance than the two **parallel** Key and Value projections. The refined balanced slide gave the three branches equal weight and emphasized the overall conclusion instead.

## Inspection checklist

1. At first glance, can the audience name the slide's most important claim?
2. At second glance, can it identify the mechanism and compare components fairly?
3. Are line weight, color and size conveying truthful relationships?
4. Does hierarchy survive a grayscale or projection-size review?
5. Are the smallest meaningful labels still legible?
6. Are source credits available but not visually competing with the lesson?
7. In bilingual layouts, does reading order follow the intended script and diagram flow?

## Evidence discipline

Call a preference a **design judgment**, not proof of improved student learning. Record which variables changed; avoid adding new content during an A/B test. Present the chosen version, its trade-offs, and what real user/projection testing would be needed.
